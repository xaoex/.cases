1. **Improper Referral**: Instead of referring to psychiatry or neurology as medically appropriate, Plaintiff was sent to an **Ears, Nose & Throat (ENT) specialist**

3. **No Diagnostic Workup**: No psychiatric assessment was performed despite Plaintiff's specific request

4. **No Follow-up Care**: The healthcare system provided no appropriate pathway to proper diagnosis

5. **Denial of Treatment**: Years of necessary ADHD treatment were denied due to failure to diagnose

This critical failure by Swedish healthcare in 2010 had devastating cascading consequences:

1. **Delayed Diagnosis**: ADHD remained undiagnosed for minimum 6-7 years (until USA diagnosis)
2. **No Treatment Access**: Plaintiff had no access to appropriate ADHD medication or therapy
3. **Symptom Exacerbation**: Untreated ADHD symptoms likely worsened over time
4. **Workplace Vulnerability**: Made Plaintiff more vulnerable to toxic work environments
5. **Career Impact**: Untreated ADHD contributed to workplace stress and eventual breakdown
6. **Pattern Established**: First instance in a pattern of Swedish healthcare system failing Plaintiff's psychiatric needs
7. **Preventable Harm**: If properly diagnosed and treated in 2010, the subsequent breakdown may have been prevented or significantly mitigated

#### Causation and Foreseeability
- Swedish healthcare system had duty to properly evaluate psychiatric concerns
- Referral to ENT specialist for ADHD symptoms fell below standard of care
- Harm from delayed diagnosis and treatment was foreseeable
- Direct causal link between 2010 failure and subsequent workplace difficulties
