# Evidence Documentation

## Purpose

This directory contains documentation and cataloging of all evidence related to legal cases and proceedings.

## Organization

Evidence should be organized by:
1. **Case or incident** - Group evidence by the case it relates to
2. **Type of evidence** - Documents, recordings, physical items, etc.
3. **Date acquired** - Chronological organization
4. **Relevance** - Primary vs. supporting evidence

## Evidence Categories

### Documentary Evidence

- Contracts and agreements
- Correspondence (emails, letters, messages)
- Financial records (invoices, receipts, bank statements)
- Official records (government documents, certificates)
- Business records (policies, procedures, reports)

### Digital Evidence

- Email communications
- Text messages and chat logs
- Social media posts and screenshots
- Digital photographs
- Audio and video recordings
- Computer files and metadata
- Website archives

### Physical Evidence

- Objects and materials
- Photographs of physical items
- Damaged property
- Original signed documents

### Testimonial Evidence

- Witness statements
- Affidavits
- Depositions
- Expert reports

## Chain of Custody

For each piece of evidence, maintain:

1. **Date and time obtained**
2. **Person who obtained it**
3. **Original location/source**
4. **Current storage location**
5. **All transfers and access**
6. **Copies made and by whom**

Use the Evidence Tracking issue template to document chain of custody.

## Naming Convention

Use clear, descriptive file names:

```
[CASE-ID]_[DATE]_[TYPE]_[DESCRIPTION].[ext]

Example:
CASE001_2024-01-15_EMAIL_Contract-Negotiation.pdf
CASE001_2024-02-03_PHOTO_Property-Damage.jpg
CASE001_2024-03-10_AUDIO_Witness-Statement-JSmith.mp3
```

## Storage Guidelines

### Digital Storage

1. **Original files** - Store in original format
2. **Working copies** - Create copies for review/analysis
3. **Backups** - Maintain redundant backups
4. **Security** - Encrypt sensitive materials
5. **Access logs** - Track who accessed what and when

### Physical Storage

1. Store in secure, climate-controlled environment
2. Protect from damage, tampering, or loss
3. Document storage location
4. Limit access to authorized personnel
5. Create photographic record of physical evidence

## Authentication

Document how evidence is authenticated:

1. **Testimony** - Witness can identify and verify
2. **Chain of custody** - Unbroken record of possession
3. **Distinctive characteristics** - Unique features
4. **Expert analysis** - Forensic or technical verification
5. **Public records** - Official certification

## Admissibility Considerations

Before relying on evidence, consider:

- **Relevance** - Does it relate to a fact in dispute?
- **Authenticity** - Can it be properly authenticated?
- **Hearsay** - Does it contain out-of-court statements offered for truth?
- **Privilege** - Is it protected by attorney-client or other privilege?
- **Best evidence** - Is an original required?
- **Prejudicial effect** - Does unfair prejudice outweigh probative value?

Consult with legal counsel regarding admissibility questions.

## Redaction

When redacting sensitive information:

1. Create a redacted version for disclosure
2. Preserve the unredacted original
3. Document what was redacted and why
4. Follow court rules on redaction
5. Use proper redaction tools (not just highlighting)

## Spoliation Concerns

To avoid spoliation (destruction of evidence):

1. Implement legal hold procedures
2. Preserve all potentially relevant materials
3. Do not alter or destroy evidence
4. Maintain metadata and original formats
5. Document any unavoidable changes

## Review and Verification

Regularly review evidence:

1. **Completeness** - Is anything missing?
2. **Accuracy** - Is documentation correct?
3. **Organization** - Is everything properly filed?
4. **Access** - Can authorized users find what they need?
5. **Security** - Is sensitive material protected?
